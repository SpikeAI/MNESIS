Please find enclosed the manuscript entitled ``Working Memory with Polychronous Chains'' for your consideration as an original research article in \emph{Neural Networks}. I am the sole author of the work and have approved the manuscript for submission.

Storing and recalling long spatio-temporal sequences of spikes remains an open problem for spiking neural networks, because each step of a sequence depends on the network's own output at the previous step, so that small errors compound. The manuscript proposes a recurrent network of $N=1024$ leaky integrate-and-fire neurons in which every synapse carries $D=41$ learnable, heterogeneous delays. A stored pattern is encoded as a chain of overlapping spiking motifs, that is contiguous windows of $D$ time steps, each of which uniquely predicts the next activity. A closed-form initialisation, derived by deconvolving the membrane response, already gives high $F_1$ scores before any gradient step. End-to-end training with surrogate gradients through time then raises the mean $F_1$ score to approximately $0.9$ on $M=16$ patterns of $T=1000\,\mathrm{ms}$. Retrieval remains accurate when the trigger window is corrupted, with high $F_1$ scores maintained up to about $20\%$ bit-flip noise, and the network shows attractor-like retrieval dynamics.

The study lies within the scope of the journal. It combines a biologically grounded spiking model, a learning procedure based on backpropagation through time, and an analysis of retrieval dynamics, and it addresses how working memory can be implemented in recurrent networks of spiking neurons. The results bear on both theoretical neuroscience and neuromorphic machine learning, where heterogeneous delays offer a route to temporal memory with a small parameter budget per synapse.
