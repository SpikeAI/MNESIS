\caption{Robustness of working-memory retrieval to three types of trigger-window perturbation ($F_1$ score averaged over $M=16$ patterns; each point is the mean over $11$ independent realisations of the perturbed trigger, and error bars span the $5\%$ to $95\%$ quantiles of a beta distribution fitted to these realisations). \emph{Left:} Bit-flip noise applied to all spikes in the trigger window ($p_\mathrm{flip} \in [0,1]$); the network maintains high $F_1$ up to $p_\mathrm{flip} \approx 0.2$. \emph{Middle:} Truncated trigger window (trigger duration from $0$ to $D-1 = 40$ steps); retrieval remains reliable for durations above roughly $32$ steps. \emph{Right:} Partial neuron coverage (number of silenced neurons from $0$ to $N = 1024$); the $F_1$ score degrades smoothly as more neurons are silenced and drops sharply once the majority of the trigger population is inactive. Orange dashed lines indicate extremal $F_1$ values $0$ or $1$.}\label{fig:noise}
